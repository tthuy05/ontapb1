# Study Content and Exam Research

## Research Method

This document defines what the application should teach and simulate for Vietnam's VSTEP.3-5 Level 3 target. It separates three things that are often mixed incorrectly:

1. **official test specification** — the four-skill format and published score bands;
2. **CEFR B1 ability** — what an independent B1 learner can generally understand and do;
3. **editorial syllabus** — an original content plan for this personal application, not an official Ministry syllabus or a copied coursebook contents page.

Research prioritized Vietnamese government/university primary sources and the Council of Europe. Current provider pages were compared with the governing 2015 test-format decision. Claims not confirmed by those sources are marked unresolved. All web sources below were accessed 29 August 2026.

## B1 Standard

The target is Vietnam's VSTEP.3-5 at Level 3, informed by Council of Europe CEFR B1 descriptors. It is not Cambridge B1 Preliminary and the editorial syllabus below is not presented as an official Ministry curriculum.

### Sources deliberately not treated as authority

Commercial preparation sites, social-media posts, copied test PDFs, coursebook tables of contents, coaching claims, and unsourced “score conversion” charts may suggest practice ideas but do not establish official format or scoring. They should not be imported into the application without rights verification and an independent correctness review.

## Verified VSTEP.3-5 structure

The target is the Vietnamese six-level framework's Level 3 through the VSTEP.3-5 test, not Cambridge B1 Preliminary. Decision 729 specifies:

| Skill | Published duration | Structure | Output / scoring facts relevant to the app |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Listening | About 40 minutes, including answer transfer in the published paper format | 3 parts, 35 multiple-choice questions; short exchanges/instructions/announcements, longer conversations, then talks/lectures | Auto-score raw objective items. Exact playback/replay behavior was not verified as universal and must be configurable/labeled. |
| Reading | 60 minutes | 4 passages, 40 multiple-choice questions; Decision 729 gives about 1,900–2,050 words total | Test gist, detail, opinion/attitude, inference, reference and meaning in context. Use the statutory length for strict simulations pending resolution of provider-page conflict. |
| Writing | 60 minutes | Task 1 letter/email of about 120 words; Task 2 essay of about 250 words | Task 1 contributes one third and Task 2 two thirds of the published Writing result. MVP records practice and self-checks, not official examiner scoring. |
| Speaking | About 12 minutes | Part 1 social interaction; Part 2 discuss solutions and choose/justify the best; Part 3 develop a topic, with follow-up discussion/questions | Browser-local rehearsal. Exact preparation timing and center interface are provider details until verified. |

### Published result bands

Each skill is reported on a 0–10 scale rounded to 0.5. The four-skill mean is likewise rounded to 0.5. Decision 729 reports:

- 4.0–5.5: Level 3;
- 6.0–8.0: Level 4;
- 8.5–10: Level 5;
- below 4.0: no level reported.

No authoritative public table converting a count of correct Listening/Reading answers into the 0–10 skill score was verified in this research. The app therefore stores points, correct count and percentage and must not manufacture a VSTEP skill score or Level 3 result. No official per-skill minimum was found in the cited format decision; one must not be invented.

### Known conflicts and unknowns

- Reading total: Decision 729 says 1,900–2,050 words; the current ULIS format page says 1,900–2,500. Strict mock validation uses 1,900–2,050 and displays a research note until the target test provider confirms otherwise.
- Detailed examiner rubrics/analytic weights for Writing and Speaking were not verified in a current, public, controlling source. CEFR-informed checklists are learning aids only.
- Official raw-answer conversion, listening replay policy, exact speaking preparation timings, permitted note-taking and navigation/interface rules can vary or change and require provider-specific verification.
- The current ULIS evidence is computer-delivered, but the app must not claim that every authorized VSTEP site uses identical delivery behavior.

## CEFR B1 learning interpretation

The 2020 Companion Volume supports the following product-level interpretation. It is paraphrased and must stay distinct from official VSTEP scoring.

| Area | B1 learner capability | Content implication |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | Understand main points and relevant detail in clear standard speech on familiar everyday, study and work topics, including short narratives | Start with clear speech and familiar contexts; progress to longer turns, distractors, attitude and note-selecting |
| Reading | Read straightforward factual texts on familiar/interest-related subjects with satisfactory comprehension | Use notices, messages, articles, narratives and practical information; test gist, scan, inference, reference and vocabulary in context |
| Spoken interaction | Communicate with some confidence on familiar routine and non-routine matters; exchange/check information; manage common travel situations; enter unprepared familiar conversation; explain opinions | Frequent personal questions, clarification language, solution comparison, reasons and follow-up questions |
| Spoken production | Sustain a reasonably fluent, straightforward description as a connected linear sequence | Use signposting and simple paragraph-like organization for experiences, plans, comparisons and a developed topic |
| Written production | Write straightforward connected texts on familiar subjects by linking shorter elements in a linear sequence | Practice clear purpose, paragraphs, sequencing, reasons/examples and basic register control |
| Vocabulary | Have a good range for familiar topics, everyday life and interests, using circumlocution when needed | Teach high-utility words, chunks, collocations, word families and repair/paraphrase strategies, not isolated rare lists |
| Grammar | Maintain reasonable accuracy in familiar contexts; errors occur but the intended meaning is usually clear | Prioritize control of frequent patterns and editing for high-impact errors before advanced structures |
| Fluency/control | Keep communication going despite pauses or reformulation; elementary vocabulary is generally controlled though complex ideas cause errors | Timed retrieval, planning, self-repair and intelligibility matter more than accent imitation or ornate language |

### Vocabulary item quality standard

Each active item should normally have a concise English meaning, part of speech, B1-natural example, one or two useful collocations/chunks, topic, source/license record, and optional Vietnamese note. Pronunciation can use owner-created audio or a legally permitted source. Examples must be original or licensed, not copied from dictionaries without permission.

## Grammar syllabus

“Required” below means needed for confident B1 communication and common VSTEP tasks; it does not imply a Ministry checklist.

| Unit | Learning objective / required control | Frequent problems to diagnose | Practice | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sentence structure and questions | Control basic word order, negatives, yes/no and wh- questions | auxiliary omission; subject/verb order; fragments | reorder, error correction, Part 1 questions | High |
| Present simple and continuous | Distinguish routines/states from temporary/current actions | stative continuous; missing third-person `-s` | context choice, routine description | High |
| Past simple and continuous | Narrate events and background in sequence | tense switching; irregular forms; `when/while` | narrative gaps, story listening/retell | High |
| Present perfect vs past simple | Connect experience/recent result to now and separate finished time | finished-time markers; `for/since`; participles | timeline choice, experience interview | High |
| Future forms | Use `will`, `be going to`, present continuous/simple for predictions/plans/schedules | one future form for all meanings | planning dialogue, predictions, email arrangements | High |
| Modal verbs | Express ability, permission, obligation, prohibition, advice, possibility and deduction | modal + inflected verb; strength confusion | notices, advice, solution comparison | High |
| Nouns, articles and quantifiers | Control countability, articles, amount and common determiners | zero/article misuse; `much/many`; uncountables | editing, shopping/service contexts | High |
| Pronouns and determiners | Maintain clear reference with subject/object/possessive/reflexive/indefinite forms | ambiguous reference; possessive confusion | reference questions, sentence repair | Medium |
| Adjectives and comparison | Describe and compare with comparative/superlative, `as…as`, modifiers | double comparison; adjective order | product/place comparison, Part 2 | High |
| Adverbs | Express manner, frequency, degree and sentence stance | adjective/adverb form; placement | transformation, listening detail | Medium |
| Prepositions | Use high-frequency time/place/movement and dependent patterns | direct translation; verb/adjective combinations | chunks, maps, gap context | High |
| Gerunds and infinitives | Control common verb/adjective patterns and purpose | form after preposition; meaning-changing verbs | pattern sorting, error correction | High |
| Relative clauses | Add identifying/non-identifying information with clear reference | missing/extra pronoun; `who/which/where` | combine sentences, reading reference | High |
| Conditionals 0, 1 and 2 | Explain facts, likely consequences and hypothetical solutions | tense in `if` clause; will/would confusion | consequences, advice, problem solving | High |
| Passive voice | Focus action/result in common present/past/modals | missing `be`; wrong participle; needless passive | notices/process/news transformation | Medium |
| Reported speech | Report statements/questions/requests with practical backshift and reference changes | question order; pronoun/time changes | dialogue reporting, news summary | Medium |
| Conjunctions and complex clauses | Link contrast, reason, result, purpose, time and concession | comma splices; connector meaning/register | sentence combining, paragraph cohesion | High |
| Past habits and change | Use `used to`, past routines and comparison with now | `be/get used to` confusion | personal change speaking/writing | Medium |
| `too`, `enough`, `so`, `such` | Express degree and consequence accurately | form and noun/adjective pattern confusion | transformation, problem description | Medium |
| Editing for agreement and form | Detect subject–verb agreement, tense/form, plurals and punctuation | locally understandable but repeated basic errors | timed proofreading and error log | High |

Every lesson follows: diagnostic example → short explanation → controlled recognition → meaningful sentence production → skill-integrated task → error-log review. Grammar exercises should not dominate the product; grammar is reinvested in Reading, Listening, Writing and Speaking.

## Reading content plan

### Skill sequence

1. locate explicit detail and scan practical texts;
2. identify main idea/purpose and paragraph function;
3. infer word/phrase meaning from context;
4. track pronoun/reference and cohesion;
5. distinguish fact, opinion, tone and attitude;
6. infer unstated meaning and writer stance;
7. manage longer four-passage timed sets.

### Text portfolio

Use original or clearly licensed emails, notices, webpages, short reports, narratives, biographies, explanatory articles and balanced opinion pieces across the vocabulary topics. Training starts around 250–500 words per passage so one skill can be isolated, then increases. A strict full Reading simulation uses four passages, 40 questions and the Decision 729 total of about 1,900–2,050 words until the official discrepancy is resolved.

For every passage store purpose/genre, topic, approximate word count, editorial B1 difficulty (`easy/core/stretch`), subskills, source/license, answer rationale, and reviewer status. Do not manufacture “past papers” or copy current-news articles wholesale.

### Reading question-bank target

Pilot: 20 passages and about 120 reviewed items. Balanced full bank: at least 50 passages and 400 items, with no passage used so often that answers are memorized. Approximate item balance: explicit detail 25%, main idea/purpose 15%, vocabulary in context 15%, reference/cohesion 10%, inference 20%, attitude/opinion/organization 15%. These are editorial balance targets, not official weights.

## Listening content plan

### Skill sequence

1. understand gist, speaker purpose and situation in short exchanges;
2. select key factual details such as time, place, number, reason and next action;
3. follow longer conversation turns, agreement/disagreement and attitude;
4. follow signposted talks, narratives and short lectures;
5. infer intention and distinguish distractors/paraphrases;
6. take minimal notes and manage a timed three-part set.

### Audio portfolio

- Short: two-speaker everyday exchanges, instructions and announcements.
- Medium: service, travel, study and workplace conversations/interviews.
- Long: one-speaker talks, stories, explanations and accessible lecture-style material.
- Speech: clear and natural rather than artificially slow, with progressive density. Use multiple intelligible speakers and accents, while maintaining B1 accessibility; the official sources reviewed do not mandate one accent set or exact words per minute.
- Transcript: retained for QA and revealed in study/review according to exercise policy, not embedded in pre-submit attempt HTML.

Prefer owner-recorded audio from original scripts with recorded speaker consent. Next-best sources are public-domain or open-licensed audio whose derivative/reuse terms permit the application. Record license and attribution per asset. Do not use commercial course audio, broadcasts, podcasts or synthetic-voice service outputs unless their terms explicitly allow the intended storage and reuse.

Pilot: 30 audio pieces and about 120 reviewed items, deliberately small enough for the host quota. Full bank target: 90 pieces and 350–450 items. Encode for intelligibility at conservative file size and verify monthly transfer. Replay policy is configurable and clearly labeled because a universal current official rule was not verified.

## Writing content plan

### Task 1 — correspondence

Practice approximately 120-word letters/emails with a clear purpose and all requested points: requests/enquiries, invitations and replies, advice, explanation/apology, complaint, arrangements and experience/news. Vary relationship/register (friend, service, organizer, teacher/work context) without demanding advanced formal style.

### Task 2 — essay

Practice approximately 250-word connected essays: opinion, discuss perspectives, advantages/disadvantages, problem/solution and cause/effect where compatible with the provider prompt. Topics come from the high-priority syllabus and remain accessible without specialist knowledge.

### Production cycle

1. identify recipient/purpose/position and required content;
2. make a short paragraph plan;
3. draft within time;
4. check content completion, organization/linking, vocabulary range/control, grammar/accuracy and mechanics;
5. revise one or two high-impact issues and record recurring errors.

The five self-check areas are CEFR-informed editorial aids, not a verified official analytic rubric. The UI notes the published Task 1/Task 2 one-third/two-thirds weighting but does not calculate an official Writing score.

Pilot inventory: 24 Task 1 and 24 Task 2 original prompts, distributed by topic/purpose. Each prompt needs task type, audience, context, bullet requirements, target length, suggested time, planning cues, sample outline, source status and reviewer state. Model answers, if included later, should be original annotated examples rather than a single “perfect” response to memorize.

## Speaking content plan

### Part 1 — social interaction

Question sets cover home, study/work, routines, transport, health, technology, leisure, local area, past experiences and plans. Train direct answer + reason/example + natural follow-up, not scripted monologues.

### Part 2 — solution discussion

Give a practical situation and usually several plausible options. Practice defining the goal, comparing advantages/disadvantages, choosing one, justifying it, and explaining why alternatives are less suitable. Exact prompt layout is content metadata, not hard-coded globally.

### Part 3 — topic development

Use a familiar issue with prompt ideas. Practice a clear opening, two or three developed points with reason/example, transitions, conclusion and unscripted follow-up questions.

### Self-review

After local playback, review task completion/development, organization/discourse, vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation/intelligibility, fluency and interaction. These categories support learning but are not presented as a current official VSTEP examiner rubric. Encourage comprehensibility, repair and connected speech; do not demand a native accent.

Pilot inventory: 40 Part 1 sets, 30 Part 2 scenarios and 30 Part 3 topics, with optional follow-up questions. Browser recording uses HTTPS permission and codec feature detection, stays local, and offers explicit download if the owner wants to keep it.

## Integrated weekly study strategy

A sustainable default is five focused days plus one mock/review block and one rest/catch-up day. It is adjustable, not a medical or official prescription.

| Session | Suggested 45–75 minute composition |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary/grammar | retrieval review; one small new batch; controlled practice; 5–10 minutes using it in speech/writing |
| Reading | one subskill drill; timed passage; evidence-based review of every distractor; add only useful language to review |
| Listening | first listen under exercise policy; answer; second analytical listen/transcript check in review; brief spoken summary |
| Writing | alternate Task 1 and Task 2; plan/draft/self-check; rewrite one weak paragraph or error pattern |
| Speaking | Parts 1/2/3 rotation; timed local recording; playback/self-review; repeat once with one target improvement |
| Weekly mixed block | full skill section or periodic mock; update error log and next-week focus from topic/type breakdown |

Use a diagnostic first, then allocate more time to the weakest skill while maintaining all four. Practice in two modes: **learning mode** with explanations/transcripts/retries and **simulation mode** with fixed time/no feedback until submission. Progress evidence should be repeated task performance and error reduction, not only time spent.

## Question-bank governance

### Required editorial metadata

Every active item should have skill, topic, type, editorial difficulty, learning objective/subskill, source category/reference/license, author/reviewer status, explanation/rationale, and last review date. Reading/listening items also link to their source content and identify evidence/transcript location in private editorial notes.

### Workflow

`draft → content review → answer/rationale review → source/license review → active → inactive/revise`

One owner may perform all roles at different times, but the checklist remains separate. Any changed correct answer or source content creates a new review need. Historical attempts are safe through snapshots; future attempts use the updated active version.

### Quality checklist

- One unambiguously best answer for single-choice/true-false.
- Distractors are plausible from the text/audio but demonstrably wrong, not trick grammar unrelated to the target.
- Question is answerable from supplied material/common task context, without specialist or culturally obscure knowledge.
- Language and cognitive load fit B1 target, with stretch explicitly labeled.
- Explanation identifies evidence and why distractors fail.
- No copied wording beyond legally permitted quotation; source/license record is complete.
- Counts, ordering, audio, and display are manually previewed before activation.

## Copyright and licensing strategy

The safest application content is original text, scripts, prompts, explanations and owner-recorded audio. Public-domain and open-license material may be used only after checking the exact work, license version, attribution, modification/share-alike/noncommercial terms and compatibility with hosting. A public webpage being readable does not grant reuse rights.

Official documents are references for facts and structure; do not copy whole test items, rubrics, sample papers, provider branding or interface assets into the bank. Commercial textbooks/prep sites are research leads only unless separately licensed. Record provenance in the schema before activation. If rights are uncertain, store a bibliographic link/note, write a new original item, or exclude the content.

## Content MVP recommendation

The technical MVP should ship with a deliberately small, balanced, entirely original/open-licensed pilot:

- 12 high-priority vocabulary topics, about 250–350 reviewed entries;
- the 12–15 highest-priority grammar units with integrated exercises;
- 20 reading passages / about 120 items;
- 30 compact listening assets / about 120 items, subject to measured quota;
- 48 writing prompts split evenly across Tasks 1 and 2;
- 100 speaking prompt sets/scenarios/topics across the three parts;
- several section practices and one clearly labeled VSTEP-style full mock assembled only after all format checks pass.

Content creation is a controlled implementation/content phase after approval, not work performed in Phase 1.
